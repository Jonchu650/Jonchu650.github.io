§ 01  ·  The machine

How Roblox decides who sees your game

Two stages, eight signals, one hard rule about which players count. Everything you do to grow a game is ultimately an attempt to move a number on this page.

01Retrieval and ranking

The Recommended for You sort on Home is where the large majority of Roblox players find new games. It runs in two stages.

StageWhat happensWhat influences it
1. RetrievalThe algorithm narrows millions of games down to a candidate set this particular user might enjoy.Broad signals of engagement, retention and monetization. Crucially, any traffic source can get you considered here — sponsored ads, curation, search, charts, friends, teleports, notifications and off-platform sharing all accelerate consideration. Even a small number of players signals that a game is worth testing more widely.
2. RankingThe candidate set is ordered personally for that user. How far your game travels is decided here.The eight signals below — and only as measured on users who organically arrived from Recommended for You.
This asymmetry is the most useful structural fact about the system. Paid and social traffic buys you an audition; only organic behaviour wins the part.

02The organic-only rule

Roblox states it plainly: it does not count the engagement, monetization or retention of users first acquired from ads, curation, friends, search, social media or any other source in the ranking stage.

Three consequences follow, and most creators get at least one of them wrong:

  • Your dashboard averages and your ranking inputs are different numbers. Your overall D1 retention blends every source. The algorithm reads a subset. Use the Home Recommendations tab in the Acquisition dashboard to see the subset that matters.
  • A successful ad campaign can coincide with falling impressions. It is not a contradiction. The ads brought players who do not count; if organic behaviour was unchanged or worse, organic distribution does not grow.
  • Paid traffic is for getting retrieved, and for revenue. Both are legitimate reasons to buy traffic. “Boosting the algorithm” is not one of them.
The one exception worth knowing

Retrieval genuinely does respond to outside traffic. For a brand-new game with no history, a coordinated push — a few creators, a TikTok that lands, a sponsored burst — is how you get into the candidate pool at all. The mistake is doing this before the organic behaviour can survive the audition.

03The eight signals, in depth

Listed in Roblox's published priority order. Each entry gives the definition, what moving it actually requires, and the trap specific to it.

01   Play through rate

Most importantNo time segments

The rate at which users play your game after seeing it in the Recommended for You sort.

Measured on
Impressions from Home recommendations only
Moved by
Thumbnail, icon, title, and how well all three predict the actual game
Levers
Thumbnail personalisation (2–5 active thumbnails), icon clarity, a title that states the loop
Trap
It drops temporarily whenever impressions rise, because you are being shown to a less-targeted audience. Do not panic-revert a thumbnail during a growth spike.

This is the only signal decided before anyone plays. Roblox reports that games using thumbnail personalisation saw an average +8.5% qualified play through rate, with some games up +50%. Personalisation only activates with two or more active thumbnails, and Roblox recommends keeping several active permanently rather than picking a winner — the algorithm keeps re-testing as audiences shift.

02   First play bounce rate

Most importantNegative signal

The rate at which users leave your game after a short first session. High is bad.

Measured in
Two buckets: under 60 seconds, and 61–180 seconds
Moved by
Load time, the first thing on screen, the first 30 seconds of gameplay
Levers
Cut load time, cut the tutorial, put the core loop's payoff inside the first minute
Trap
Under-60s bounces are usually technical, not creative — slow streaming, a broken first frame, a crash on low-memory Android.

Split your thinking by bucket. A player who leaves in under a minute rarely saw your game; they saw a loading screen, a wall of UI, or a device that could not handle it. A player who leaves at two minutes saw it and was not convinced. The two failures have completely different fixes, which is exactly why Roblox measures them separately.

03   Play days per user

Most important

The average number of unique days users engage with your game.

Segments
D1 average · D2–7 average · D8–28 average
Moved by
Reasons to return: daily rewards, timed events, progression that resumes, social obligation
Levers
Daily login value, offline progression, unfinished goals at logout, experience notifications
Trap
Counts days, not sessions. Three sessions in one day is one play day. Spreading engagement across days beats deepening it within a day.

04   Playtime per user

Most important60 min/day cap

The average amount of time users spend in your game, capped at 60 minutes per user, per game, per day.

Segments
D1 average · D2–7 average · D8–28 average
Moved by
Session structure, pacing, whether there is a natural next thing to do
Levers
Goals that end just past the current session, group content, queues that fill quickly
Trap
The 60-minute cap means grinding your most devoted players harder is worth nothing above the cap. Every minute of value is in moving a 4-minute player to 15, not a 90-minute player to 120.
The cap is a strategy

The 60-minute cap converts “maximise engagement” into a much more specific and much more achievable instruction: raise the floor, not the ceiling. Find the distribution of session lengths, not the average. Your work is at the bottom of that distribution.

05   Intentional co-play days per user

Important

The average number of unique days users come back to play your game with friends — via join, invites, or private servers.

Segments
D1 · D2–7 · D8–28
Moved by
Whether playing together is better than playing alone, and whether the game says so
Levers
In-game invite prompts, friend-bonus mechanics, shared objectives, private servers, referral rewards
Trap
“Intentional” is doing work in that name. Two strangers in the same public server is not co-play. A join, an invite, or a private server is.

This is the most under-exploited signal on the list for solo developers, because it is the one you can move with UI rather than content. Roblox ships a player invite prompt API and a friend referral reward system specifically for it.

06   Qualified play sessions per user

Important

The average number of qualified play sessions per user who clicked through from Home recommendations. A qualified play is a meaningful session, filtering out accidental clicks and quick bounces.

Segments
D1 · D2–7 · D8–28
Moved by
The same things that move bounce rate, plus reasons to start a second session
Levers
Anything that turns a first session into a habit inside the first week
Trap
Roblox does not publish the qualifying threshold. Community estimates circulate; treat them as folklore. Optimise for “obviously a real session,” not for a number someone guessed.

07   Spend days per user

Important

The average number of unique days on which users spend Robux in your game.

Segments
D1 · D2–7 · D8–28
Moved by
How often there is something worth buying, at a price a normal player can reach
Levers
Cheap consumables, daily-refreshing offers, low-price entry products, first-purchase discounts
Trap
A single 1,000 Robux gamepass generates one spend day. Ten 80 Robux consumables across ten days generate ten. Price ladders are an algorithm strategy, not just a revenue strategy.

08   Robux spent per user

Important

The average amount of Robux users spend in your game.

Segments
D1 · D2–7 · D8–28
Moved by
Payer conversion and ARPPU together
Levers
See Monetization
Trap
It is a per-user average, so whales are diluted by everyone who spends nothing. Broad conversion moves this more reliably than deep spending does.

04Explore and expand

Roblox describes the recommendation system as running explore and expand phases. After a content update you may see a spike in new users from recommendations — that is explore. If that new cohort engages and monetizes well, Roblox keeps recommending you to more cohorts like them — that is expand.

Practical reading: every content update is an audition, and the audience judging it is a cohort you have never served before. This is why updates sometimes crater retention even when long-time players love them. New users were sampled, the update was aimed at veterans, the cohort bounced. Segment your post-update numbers by When user first played before you conclude anything.

When distribution increases, you settle into a new baseline

Roblox warns explicitly that signals fluctuate while the algorithm works out who your game suits, before stabilising. Give a growth spike two to three weeks before treating its numbers as the truth. Acting on week-one data during an expansion phase is how creators talk themselves out of updates that were working.

05Why impressions move

Roblox lists four causes. Only one of them is you.

CauseWhat it looks likeWhat to do
What you doImpression change follows an update, a metadata change, or a signal movingCheck the Home Recommendation signal charts across D1, D2–7 and D8–28
What Roblox doesPlatform-wide algorithm changes, with no corresponding change in your signalsCheck whether similar games moved too. Do not chase it
Total Home trafficWeekly seasonality peaking Saturday, plus summer, back-to-school and holiday cyclesCompare week-over-week and year-over-year, never day-over-day
Other gamesYour signals are flat, impressions fall anyway — someone else got betterNothing defensive. The only answer is to improve your own per-user numbers

06What suppresses exposure

Roblox names three content-quality issues that reduce visibility on Home and in search, independent of your metrics.

Issue 01

Leading with giveaways

Metadata implying a monetary reward is deprioritised. A game titled “Robux! Play now!” gets less exposure because the title leads with money instead of gameplay.

Issue 02

Mismatched metadata

Thumbnails and titles that do not match the actual game are not recommended and rank lower in search. Dinosaurs on the tile, a generic obby in the game.

Issue 03

Non-unique games

Metadata and place files closely resembling existing games are no longer prioritised. Cloning a trending title's name and visuals is now a penalty, not a shortcut.

Check whether you are flagged

Games with reduced exposure show a banner on the Creator Dashboard that updates daily. Roblox reclassifies content quality with every update, so the penalty is recoverable — align with the best practices and republish. If you believe it is wrong, file a Bug Report through Support with your Universe ID.

The line between trend-following and cloning

Roblox's own guidance is to add your own spin to existing trends in title, images, description and in-game content. Building in a proven genre is fine and always has been. Shipping something whose name and tile could be confused for the game it is imitating is now measurably costly.

08Using the Home Recommendations dashboard

Analytics › Acquisition › Home Recommendations is the only place the ranking inputs are visible. Work it in this order when impressions drop:

  1. Confirm the drop is real and date it against your release history and the platform's weekly seasonality.
  2. Check the four most-important signals first. Roblox states the typical pattern explicitly: an impression drop usually follows a fall in play through rate, play days per user and playtime per user, and a rise in first play bounce rate.
  3. Read all three time windows. If D1 is stable but D2–7 and D8–28 fell, your onboarding is fine and your retention past the first day broke — look at progression, not the tutorial.
  4. If the top four are stable, move to the second tier — co-play days, qualified sessions, spend days, Robux spent.
  5. Compare against the similar-games benchmark shown at the bottom of the page to find which signal is furthest below par. That is your opportunity, not your emergency.
Benchmarks do not feed the algorithm

Roblox says this twice in its own documentation, which suggests people keep assuming otherwise. The benchmark set exists to give you a point of comparison. Beating it does not rank you; it just tells you that a given signal is no longer your weakest link.
