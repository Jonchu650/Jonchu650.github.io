§ 00  ·  Orientation

Eight numbers decide whether anyone sees your game

Roblox hands you something like sixty metrics across nine dashboards. Exactly eight of them feed the recommendation algorithm, and they are only counted for players who found you organically. This manual works outward from those eight.

Everything else on the Creator Hub is diagnostic: it exists to tell you why one of the eight moved. That distinction is the whole game. Chase the eight; use the rest to explain them.

Read this first

The eight signals below are measured only on users who organically joined from the Recommended for You sort on Home. Engagement, retention and spend from ads, search, charts, friends, teleports or a TikTok link do not count toward ranking. You can buy players. You cannot buy the signal.

01The signal ladder

This is Roblox's own published priority order for the Recommended for You sort — the sort that supplies most of the traffic on the platform. It runs in two stages: retrieval picks a candidate set of games you might like, then ranking orders them. Almost everything you control lives in the ranking stage.

How to read the time segments

Six of the eight signals are measured separately across D1, D2–7 and D8–28 windows. That means a game can look healthy on day one and still lose distribution because its D8–28 numbers decayed. When impressions drop, check all three windows before you touch anything — the window that moved tells you whether your problem is onboarding, progression, or endgame.

02Three things that follow from the ladder

Averages, not totals

Every signal is a per-user average. A game with 400 devoted players can out-rank a game with 40,000 bored ones. Roblox states this explicitly to reassure small creators, but the sharper implication is the inverse: a flood of low-intent players actively drags your ranking down. Dumping cheap traffic into a game that is not ready is not neutral — it is negative.

Retention is weighted three separate ways

Play days per user, playtime per user, and qualified sessions per user are three different questions about the same behaviour: came back, stayed, and meant it. Together they outweigh everything monetary on the list. Roblox has said its explicit direction of travel is a system that “promotes games with strong long term retention.”

Money is on the list, but it is the quiet half

Spend days and Robux spent per user sit in the second tier. Note the ordering: spend days is listed before Robux spent. A game where lots of players buy something small on many days is reading better than one where a handful of whales buy something huge once.

03The loop Roblox tells you to run

Roblox publishes a three-step growth sequence, and the order is not decoration — running it out of order is the single most common way creators waste money.

Step 1

Fix the game first

Get D1 retention and average session time to or above your similar-game benchmark. Then payer conversion and ARPPU. Nothing else matters until this is true.

Step 2

Then drive acquisition

Only once retention holds. Use the Acquisition dashboard to find which sources convert and which ones bring players who stay.

Step 3

Then measure every update

After each release, check D1 and D7 retention, session time, payer conversion, ARPPU, revenue, new users and play through rate.

The expensive mistake

Running step 2 before step 1. Ads and influencer pushes send players into a leaky game; they bounce; your bounce rate and per-user averages get worse; the algorithm demotes you. You pay money to reduce your own organic distribution. Roblox's docs say to sequence it this way; the ranking mechanics explain why.

04The arithmetic underneath all of it

Two identities do most of the explanatory work. Learn to decompose any change into these terms and you will almost always find the cause.

Revenue

Revenue  =  DAU  ×  ARPDAU
         =  DAU  ×  payer conversion  ×  ARPPU

Revenue only ever moves for one of three reasons: fewer people, a smaller share of them buying, or buyers spending less. Check which one before you redesign the shop.

Audience size

DAU  ≈  average CCU  ×  1440  ÷  average daily minutes per user

A game averaging 5,000 concurrent players with 30 minutes of daily playtime per user is serving roughly 240,000 DAU. Use average CCU, not peak — peak typically runs well above average. This is the identity to use when you want to sanity-check a revenue-per-CCU rule of thumb you read somewhere, rather than trusting the rule of thumb.
