§ 12  ·  Practice

Diagnostics

Something moved and you need to know why. Six decision trees, ordered by how often each cause turns out to be the real one.

Before any of these: the two-minute triage

1. Date it. Line the change up against your publish history and Roblox's update annotations on the Performance dashboard.
2. Check seasonality. Weekly traffic peaks Saturday and dips midweek; there are summer, back-to-school and holiday cycles. Compare week-over-week, never day-over-day.
3. Check your filters. Dashboard filters persist across pages until you turn them off. A forgotten filter explains more ‘anomalies’ than anything else on this page.

Six diagnostics
  1. Home impressions dropped
  2. Retention dropped after an update
  3. Session time dropped
  4. Revenue dropped
  5. Play through rate dropped
  6. Nothing dropped, but nothing is growing

01Home impressions dropped

Go to Acquisition › Home Recommendations. Roblox states the usual pattern outright: an impression drop typically follows a fall in play through rate, play days per user and playtime per user, and a rise in first play bounce rate.

Do not forget the fourth cause

Roblox lists four drivers of impression change and only one is you. If your signals are flat and there is no quality banner, the honest answer is often that somebody else got better. There is no defensive move — only offence.

02Retention dropped after an update

  1. Segment by When user first played. This single step resolves most cases.
    • Only the 0–30 day cohort fell → you changed the new-player experience.
    • Only older cohorts fell → you changed something veterans depended on, or your update was aimed at newcomers and gave existing players nothing.
    • Everyone fell → likely technical. Go to the performance dashboard.
  2. Check whether new users spiked on the same date. A large influx of new users temporarily depresses retention on all three charts. Roblox calls this out as a typical and temporary outcome. Your retention did not get worse — your mix got more casual.
  3. Check the new user first session retention curve against the previous period to find the minute where you started losing people.
  4. Open the onboarding funnel and compare completion rates step by step, with the date range starting from the update. Remember to check whether you changed the funnel steps themselves — Roblox warns on affected steps if your range spans an edit.
  5. Check error and performance reports for the same window.

If all of that is clean, it is a design change: something you added is being skipped, or something you removed was holding people. Segment by cohort age and look at what changed for that group.

04Revenue dropped

Decompose before you investigate. Revenue = DAU × conversion × ARPPU. Exactly one of the three usually moved.

Term that fellMeaningWhere to look
DAUA traffic problem wearing a revenue costumeAcquisition and impressions — diagnostic 01
Payer conversionFewer people are starting to spendShop funnel, shop visibility, price of your cheapest item, whether a first-purchase offer broke
ARPPUSpenders are going less deepCatalogue breadth, whether a popular item was removed or nerfed, seasonal item expiry

Then check these three

  • Compare against similar games. Roblox's insights panel does this for 100+ DAU games. A 21% revenue decline while similar games rose 15% is a real problem; a 21% decline while everyone fell is seasonality.
  • Break down by Active Payer Status. If the top 15% segment collapsed, you lost whales — a retention problem among your most invested players, not a pricing problem.
  • Check average wallet balance. Rising balances mean players have currency and nothing they want. That reads as a revenue problem and is actually a content problem.

05Play through rate dropped

First, check whether impressions rose immediately beforehand. Roblox states that PTR dips temporarily when impressions increase, because you are being shown to a broader, less-targeted audience. This is normal and it stabilises.

If impressions did not rise:

  • Did you change your icon, title, thumbnails or description? Revert and observe.
  • Did thumbnail personalisation get switched off, or drop below two active thumbnails? It needs at least two to run.
  • Is there a content-quality banner on your Creator Dashboard?
  • Has a similar game launched with a near-identical tile? Non-uniqueness cuts both ways — you may now look like the copy.
  • Check PTR by source. A drop confined to one source is a placement change, not a creative problem.

06Nothing dropped, but nothing is growing

The most common situation, and the least well served by dashboards, because nothing is flagged as wrong. Work through the ladder in order and find your weakest signal relative to benchmark.

Check 1

Is your weakest signal below the 50th percentile?

Compare each signal to the similar-games benchmark on the Home Recommendations page. Fix the one furthest below median — not the one you find most interesting.

Check 2

Is your bounce rate the real problem?

It is the second-highest-weighted signal and the most commonly ignored, because no dashboard puts it on the front page. Look at the under-60s bucket specifically.

Check 3

Do you have any second-tier signal at all?

Many games have effectively zero co-play design and no cheap repeatable purchase. Those are two of the four second-tier signals sitting at zero.

Check 4

Are you shipping on a cadence?

Roblox's published rhythm is small updates every 2–4 weeks and large ones every 2–3 months. A flat game is often just a game that stopped being updated.

The order to attack a plateau

Bounce rate first (fastest to move, second-highest weight). Then median session length toward 10 minutes. Then play days, via a return hook. Then co-play, via invite prompts. Then a cheap repeatable purchase for spend days. That sequence works because each step is cheaper than the one after it and feeds a signal the previous step did not.

07The post-update routine

Roblox's own list of what to check after every update, plus the segmentation that makes it useful:

  • D1 and D7 retention — segmented by When user first played
  • Average session time — broken down by platform and place version
  • Payer conversion, ARPPU and revenue
  • New users and play through rate
  • Crash rate and out-of-memory exits — against the previous place version
  • Feedback comments from the 48 hours after release, with the device column visible
  • The Home Recommendation signals across D1, D2–7 and D8–28, about a week later
