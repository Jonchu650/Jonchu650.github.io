§ 13  ·  Playbook

The growth playbook

What to do, in order, from before launch to past a thousand daily players. Each phase has a different bottleneck and a different set of numbers that are even visible to you.

The organising principle

Roblox's published sequence is: fix retention and engagement, then monetization, then acquisition, then measure every update. The reason it is an order and not a list is that the ranking signals are per-user averages — so acquiring players into a game that does not hold them actively lowers your organic distribution. Every phase below is a version of “do not skip ahead.”

00Pre-launch

Bottleneck: you have no data and no benchmarks, and the only analytics surface available to you is the one you build yourself.

Build

  • The day-one instrumentation kit. Onboarding funnel, shop funnel, economy events, a progression event, a core-loop event, and a session-length event. See Instrumentation.
  • Reserve custom field 1 for your content variant — map, class, pet type, whatever your game varies. Every chart becomes splittable by it for free.
  • 2–5 thumbnails, all activated. Personalisation does not run below two. This is the cheapest play-through-rate gain available and it requires no code.
  • A title that states the loop. The best-performing Roblox titles function as the pitch and the tutorial at once.
  • Verified email and 2-step verification on the account. Required for the analytics dashboard and for sales data downloads.

Test

  • Play it on a cheap Android phone. Not an emulator. Roughly a plurality of your players will use both mobile and PC, and mobile is where bounce rates are made.
  • Time yourself from join to first genuine payoff. If it is over five minutes, cut something.
  • Check nothing heavy runs on PlayerAdded. The first 60 seconds belong to asset loading.
  • Verify events actually fire. They only work from the server, in a published game — never from Studio. Publish to a test place and confirm data arrives within 24 hours.
The most expensive pre-launch mistake

Lining up a launch-day marketing push before the game holds anyone. Paid and social traffic does not feed the ranking stage, and it will drag your per-user averages down while it runs. Spend launch traffic after you know your D1 is respectable, not before.

01Launch week — days 1 to 7

Bottleneck: almost everything is noise. Your dashboard needs more than 10 DAU and 10 play hours for 7 consecutive days before full KPIs unlock, and benchmarks need 100 DAU.

WatchWhyIgnore
Crash rate and OOM exitsThe only genuinely urgent thing. A crash on a device class you did not test can silently cap your ceilingRevenue

The one thing to do this week

Find your largest onboarding funnel drop-off and fix it. In Roblox's own reference example the drop between step 1 and step 2 was 70%, and the fix was contextual prompts telling players what to do. This is almost always the highest-return change available in week one, and it costs an afternoon.

02Days 8 to 30 — first real diagnosis

Bottleneck: you now have D1 and D7 data and, if you crossed 100 DAU, benchmarks and insights. This is where you find out what kind of problem you have.

If D1 is weakbelow your benchmark's 50th percentile

Your core loop or first-time user experience is the problem. Cut the tutorial, move the payoff earlier, give starter items and currency so the loop is reachable immediately, and check performance on low-end devices. Re-check the funnel after each change.

If D1 is fine but D7 is weakplayers come back once, then stop

Your progression system is the problem. Players had a good first session and could not picture a second week. Measure the distribution of players across progression levels; if everyone is stalled at the same gate, that gate is too expensive.

If retention is fine but nothing growssignals healthy, impressions flat

Play through rate or reach. Review thumbnails, title and icon; confirm personalisation is running; check for a content-quality banner. This is also the point where a modest, targeted acquisition push becomes reasonable — because now the game holds people.

If everything is weakno signal is near benchmark

Do not optimise. The game is not working, and analytics cannot fix a game nobody wants. This is a product decision, not a metrics decision — see the last section of this page.

03100 to 1,000 DAU — the optimisation loop

Bottleneck: you have benchmarks and insights but not experiments. You are optimising by cohort comparison, one change at a time.

The weekly rhythm

WhenDo

Roblox's published update cadence. The discipline that makes it work is changing one thing at a time and recording the date, so cohort comparison stays interpretable.

The order to attack signals

First

First play bounce rate

Highest weight among things you can move quickly. Split by the under-60s and 61–180s buckets — technical versus creative.

Second

Median session length past 10 minutes

Directly monetised through Creator Rewards, sits at the platform median, and the work also improves bounce rate and qualified sessions.

Third

Play days per user

A return hook: daily reward, offline progression, or logging out mid-goal. Cheap to build, feeds a top-tier signal.

Fourth

Intentional co-play days

Invite prompts, friend bonuses, shared objectives, private servers. The most under-exploited signal for solo developers because it is UI work, not content work.

Fifth

Spend days per user

A cheap, repeatable, frequently-refreshed purchase. Ten 80-Robux buys across ten days beat one 1,000-Robux gamepass for signal purposes.

Sixth

Payer conversion depth

Only once the base is wide. High ARPPU with low ARPDAU means you are living on a narrow slice of players.

041,000+ DAU — test instead of guess

Bottleneck: you can now reach statistical significance, and the cost of a wrong decision has risen enough to justify the wait.

  • Switch from cohort comparison to experiments. Check the MDE before committing — if it is above roughly 100%, the test cannot succeed at your scale.
  • Use configs for anything you might want to revert without publishing: progression rates, reward values, prices.
  • Read the weekly and monthly AI reports. The automatic outlier detection is the genuinely useful part — it finds segment-specific regressions you would only catch by filtering twenty combinations by hand.
  • Now paid acquisition makes sense. The game holds people, so bought traffic converts to revenue and to retrieval-stage consideration. Judge campaigns on a 30-day window and check the Recent and Resurrected user views, not just All Users.
  • Make one share link per channel and per creator so you can compare D7 retention and 30-day revenue per user across sources rather than guessing.

05A worked example

Take a simulator-style game where players collect rare objects, bring them back to a base, and the collection generates passive income — the shape of a lot of the genre right now, including Hatch a Meteor. Mapping the ladder onto it:

SignalWhat it looks like in this genreConcrete lever
Play through rateThe tile has to sell the loop in one imageThumbnail showing the collection, not the environment. Title stating the verb and the object
First play bounce ratePlayers who join and cannot tell what to doGive the first collectible free within 30 seconds. Do not make the first one a challenge
Play days per userPassive income is already a return hook — use it deliberatelyCap offline accumulation at roughly a day so there is a reason to come back tomorrow specifically
Playtime per userSessions end when there is nothing to chaseAlways have a visible next-rarity target. Telegraph incoming opportunities
Intentional co-play daysBases are inherently visitableLet players see friends' collections, and reward visiting. This is nearly free signal
Spend days per userGenre usually over-relies on expensive permanent passesAdd a cheap consumable that refreshes daily — a re-roll, a luck boost, an extra slot
Robux spent per userDriven by collection pressurePrice ladder from very cheap to aspirational, not one tier

The generalisable point is not the genre. It is that every ranking signal has a design expression, and you can write this table for any game in about twenty minutes. Doing so converts an abstract algorithm into a feature list.

06When to stop

An honest section, because the analytics are also how you decide to move on.

30 daysEnough data for D1, D7 and a first D30 read
<50thPercentile on every signal, after fixes, is the stop signal
2–3Real attempts at the weakest signal before concluding

If, after 30 days and two or three genuine attempts at your weakest signal, you are below the 50th percentile on every ranking signal and the trend is flat — the problem is the game concept, not the execution. Analytics can tell you a game is not working. It cannot tell you what game to make instead.

What carries forward: your instrumentation kit, your thumbnail testing habit, your understanding of which signals you are structurally good and bad at, and the code. That is most of the value of a failed attempt, and it is why shipping several games is a reasonable strategy and polishing one indefinitely often is not.

The thing worth internalising

Every signal in the ladder is a per-user average measured on organic players. That single sentence resolves most strategic questions: it is why cheap traffic hurts, why a 400-player game can out-rank a 40,000-player one, why the 60-minute cap means raising the floor beats raising the ceiling, and why spend frequency is worth designing for even when it is not where your revenue is.
