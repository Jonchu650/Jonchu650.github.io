Instrumentation

The only high-value analytics surface available to a game with no players yet. Three event types, all free, all server-side, and all of them unlock dashboards you cannot otherwise see.

01The three rules

Non-negotiable

Events can only be sent from the server, and only from a published game. They cannot be sent from the client, and they cannot be sent from Studio. If you are testing in Studio and seeing nothing on the dashboard, this is why. Data is aggregated daily, so allow up to 24 hours for charts to populate.

Event typeUnlocksAnswers
Funnel eventsThe Funnels pageWhere do players drop out of onboarding, progression or checkout?
Economy eventsThe Economy pageWhere do players earn and spend resources, and is the economy inflating?
Custom eventsThe Explore pageAnything specific to your game — feature adoption, weapon balance, map preference
All three are free, and none has a DAU threshold. Events remain visible on the dashboard and roll off automatically 90 days after the last data is received.

02Funnel events

A funnel is a sequence of steps with a drop-off rate between each. Roblox supports two shapes, and picking the wrong one is the most common implementation mistake.

One-time funnels — onboarding

For conversions that happen once per player. No session ID needed. This is where you should start: onboarding is where most games lose most of their users.

local AnalyticsService = game:GetService("AnalyticsService")

-- Log the first step of the first-time user experience
AnalyticsService:LogOnboardingFunnelStepEvent(
    player,
    1,               -- Step number
    "In Farm"        -- Step name
)

-- Log the second step
AnalyticsService:LogOnboardingFunnelStepEvent(
    player,
    2,
    "Plant Seed"
)

To start the funnel the moment someone joins, log step 1 on PlayerAdded:

local AnalyticsService = game:GetService("AnalyticsService")
local Players = game:GetService("Players")

Players.PlayerAdded:Connect(function(player)
    AnalyticsService:LogOnboardingFunnelStepEvent(player, 1, "Player Joined")
end)

Recurring funnels — shop, upgrades

For conversions that happen many times per player. These need a funnelSessionId to keep separate attempts apart.

local AnalyticsService = game:GetService("AnalyticsService")
local HttpService = game:GetService("HttpService")

local funnelSessionId = HttpService:GenerateGUID()

AnalyticsService:LogFunnelStepEvent(
    player,
    "ArmoryCheckout",   -- Funnel name, groups the steps together
    funnelSessionId,     -- Unique ID for this checkout attempt
    1,                   -- Step number
    "Opened Store"      -- Step name
)

AnalyticsService:LogFunnelStepEvent(player, "ArmoryCheckout", funnelSessionId, 2, "Viewed Item")
AnalyticsService:LogFunnelStepEvent(player, "ArmoryCheckout", funnelSessionId, 3, "Added to Cart")
Funnel shapeUse a funnelSessionId?What to use as the ID
One-time (onboarding)No — it only happens once per player—
Store / checkoutYesA fresh GUID each time the shop opens
Item upgrade pathsYesA stable composite key, e.g. <playerId>-<itemId>
Roblox tracks only the 10 most recent unique funnelSessionId values per user per funnel. Reusing an older one starts a new session.

Behaviour you should know before you design a funnel

  • Repeated steps are ignored. Only the first instance of a step counts. Disconnects and reconnects need no special handling for one-time funnels. But repeats still count toward your rate limit.
  • Skipped steps auto-complete. Log step 3 without 1 or 2 and the funnel marks 1 and 2 as done. Useful, but it means a bug that skips a step will silently hide a drop-off.
  • Filters apply only to the first step. This prevents double-counting when a player switches device mid-funnel — the funnel is attributed to whatever device they entered on.
  • Funnels display by cohort. A player who enters on 6/19 belongs to the 6/19 cohort even if they finish on 6/20.
  • Changing steps splits your data. After editing a funnel, set the date range to start from the edit date. Roblox warns you on the affected step if your range spans a change.
  • Be consistent about when you log. Roblox recommends logging after a player completes a step, and using names you will still understand in three months.
The one funnel every game should have

Onboarding, 4–6 steps, from PlayerAdded through the first complete pass of your core loop. In Roblox's own worked example the funnel revealed a 70% drop between “In Farm” and “Plant Seed” — players arrived and did not know what to do. The fix was contextual prompts above the flowerpots. That is a twenty-minute change found by a twenty-minute instrumentation job.

03Economy events

Every place a player gains or loses a resource is a source or a sink. Log both and the Economy dashboard becomes available.

Transaction typeDirectionExample
IAPSourceRobux exchanged for resources — a starter pack
TimedRewardSourceDaily bonus
OnboardingSourceWelcome bonus
ShopSource or sinkTrading resources in the shop
GameplaySource or sinkQuest completion; spending to play
ContextualPurchaseSinkAn impulse buy — extra lives
These are the defaults and they appear on the dashboard as-is. You can supply your own transaction type names, but start with these — after 20 unique types, the rest are grouped as “Other”.
local AnalyticsService = game:GetService("AnalyticsService")

-- SOURCE: player earns 50 coins for finishing a level
AnalyticsService:LogEconomyEvent(
    player,
    Enum.AnalyticsEconomyFlowType.Source,
    "Coins",     -- Currency name
    50,           -- Amount earned
    100,          -- Balance AFTER the transaction
    Enum.AnalyticsEconomyTransactionType.Gameplay.Name
)

-- SINK: player spends 80 coins on an upgrade
AnalyticsService:LogEconomyEvent(
    player,
    Enum.AnalyticsEconomyFlowType.Sink,
    "Coins",
    80,           -- Cost -- always POSITIVE, even for a sink
    20,           -- Balance after
    Enum.AnalyticsEconomyTransactionType.Shop.Name,
    "DoubleJumpUpgrade"   -- Item SKU (optional)
)

-- SOURCE via Robux: a 1000-coin bundle purchase
AnalyticsService:LogEconomyEvent(
    player,
    Enum.AnalyticsEconomyFlowType.Source,
    "Coins",
    1000,
    1020,
    Enum.AnalyticsEconomyTransactionType.IAP.Name,
    "1000CoinBundle"
)
Two details that cause bad data

Amounts are always positive. Even for sinks. The dashboard renders sinks as negative for you.

The fifth argument is the balance after the transaction, not before. Get this wrong and your average wallet balance chart is meaningless.

Reading the Economy dashboard

Total sources and sinks by category

Sources minus sinks should sit close to zero. If the net total is growing, your economy is inflating and you need more sinks.

Average wallet balance

Shown for all players, payers and non-payers. Rising balances — especially among payers — mean players have money and nothing they want to buy.

Top sources and sinks

If players are not sourcing from in-app purchases, your prices are too high or the options are wrong. If a resource has no meaningful sinks, add some.

04Custom events

Anything else you want to count. Two shapes: counters, and events with values.

local AnalyticsService = game:GetService("AnalyticsService")

-- Counter: a single action, no value
AnalyticsService:LogCustomEvent(player, "MissionStarted")

-- Event with a value: quantitative data
AnalyticsService:LogCustomEvent(
    player,
    "MissionCompletedDuration",
    120   -- seconds
)

Counters are treated as events with a value of 1, so max, min and average will always be 1 and the sum equals the event count. Every event automatically tracks both event count and unique user count.

Available aggregations: count, count unique user, average value, sum value, min value, max value, and average value per user — all selectable on the Explore page.

Batch values to stay under the rate limit

Sending one event with a value of 10 is far better than sending ten events. “10 zombies killed” instead of “zombie killed” ten times. This is the single most effective way to keep a busy game inside its request budget.

05Custom fields

Up to three dimensions you can break down and filter every funnel, economy and custom event by. Values must be strings.

AnalyticsService:LogEconomyEvent(
    player,
    Enum.AnalyticsEconomyFlowType.Sink,
    "Coins", 80, 20,
    Enum.AnalyticsEconomyTransactionType.Shop.Name,
    "Obsidian Sword",
    {
        [Enum.AnalyticsCustomFieldKeys.CustomField01.Name] = "Category - Weapon",
        [Enum.AnalyticsCustomFieldKeys.CustomField02.Name] = "Class - Warrior",
        [Enum.AnalyticsCustomFieldKeys.CustomField03.Name] = "Level - 10",
    }
)
Use custom fields instead of event names. Always.

Event names are capped at 100 and have a much tighter cardinality limit than custom fields, which allow 8,000 unique combinations. So instead of PlantCabbage, PlantTurnip and PlantPepper as three events, log one PlantSeed event with a custom field of Plant - Cabbage and so on.

You get the total and the per-type comparison in a single visualisation, and you have spent one of your hundred event names instead of three. The same logic applies to economy currencies: one XP currency with a class custom field, not WarriorXP, MageXP, PaladinXP.

Including the dimension name inside the value — "Class - Warrior" rather than "Warrior" — is not required, but it makes charts readable months later when you have forgotten which field was which.

Limits reset daily; stop sending and you can send again the next day. Data rolls off 90 days after the last event received.

The global rate limit scales with concurrency, which means a small game is proportionally more constrained than a large one. At 10 CCU you have 320 requests per minute across your whole game. Budget accordingly: batch values, do not log high-frequency gameplay events per-occurrence, and remember that ignored repeated funnel steps still consume quota.

07Protecting your data from exploiters

Events are server-side, but the trigger usually is not. If a client tells the server “I completed step 7,” an exploiter can tell it anything. Validate before logging.

-- Server
local AnalyticsService = game:GetService("AnalyticsService")
local ReplicatedStorage = game:GetService("ReplicatedStorage")
local onboardingEvent = ReplicatedStorage:WaitForChild("OnboardingEvent")

local maxStep = 3

local function onPlayerEventFired(player: Player, args: { step: number })
    local step = args.step
    if step > maxStep then
        warn(`Invalid tutorial step {step} received from client.`)
        return
    end
    AnalyticsService:LogOnboardingFunnelStepEvent(player, step)
end

onboardingEvent.OnServerEvent:Connect(onPlayerEventFired)

Range-check step numbers, verify the player is actually in the state the step implies, and where you can, derive the event from server-authoritative state rather than a client message at all.

08Reading segments at runtime

AnalyticsService:GetPlayerSegmentsAsync() lets you query a player's analytics segments inside your game, so you can adapt gameplay per player.

GetPlayerSegmentsAsync

Runtime

Returns segment information for a player — spending behaviour, account age in your game, and more.

Use case
Show lower-cost starter items to players who have never spent; show bundles to prior purchasers
Use case
Give extra tooltips and simplified content to players in their first 30 days; point experienced players at endgame content
Caution
Personalising prices is a different thing from personalising offers. Keep it to relevance, not discrimination

09The day-one instrumentation kit

If you are shipping a game and want the minimum viable analytics setup, this is it. It takes an afternoon and every piece of it earns out.

  • Onboarding funnel, 4–6 steps. Step 1 on PlayerAdded, final step on the first complete core-loop cycle.
  • Shop funnel with a GUID funnelSessionId: opened shop → viewed item → prompted purchase → completed.
  • Economy events on every source and sink of your primary currency, with correct post-transaction balances.
  • A progression custom event fired on level-up or tier-up, with the level as a custom field.
  • A core-loop custom event fired on each completion of your central action, with the variant as a custom field.
  • A session-length custom event logged on player removal, with the duration as the value — this gives you the distribution the dashboard will not.
  • Custom field 1 reserved for content variant (map, class, pet type), so every chart can be split by it.
  • Server-side validation on anything the client triggers.
Why this specific list

Together these five events answer the questions behind six of the eight ranking signals: where new players quit, whether the loop is rewarding, whether progression paces correctly, whether purchases complete, whether your economy inflates, and what your session-length distribution actually looks like. Everything else can wait until a number moves and you need to know why.
