§ 10  ·  Practice

Experiments & configs

The difference between “the number went up after I shipped it” and “shipping it made the number go up.” Also the tool most likely to mislead you if you use it impatiently.

01What the two tools do

Configs

Adjust values in your live game without publishing a new version. Tune progression rates, rewards, prices and gameplay parameters while servers are running.

Experiments

A/B test alternative values of a config against a control, and measure the causal impact on your real metrics with confidence intervals.

Together with analytics they form a loop: analyse to find an opportunity, change via a config or experiment, monitor the relevant metrics, repeat. Roblox's own advice is to make incremental changes rather than large ones, specifically so you can attribute the result.

How rollout percentages actually work

If you roll out to 40% of users with two variants split 50/50, then 60% of your players are excluded entirely (they get the control and are not counted), roughly 20% get the control as part of the experiment, and roughly 20% get the variant. It is easy to design a test that only measures a fifth of your audience and then wonder why nothing is significant.

Metrics every experiment tracks

Regardless of your goal metric, experiments report on all of these across the run — which is what makes them good at catching trade-offs you did not anticipate:

  • Playtime — average time spent, cumulative over the experiment
  • Session time — playtime divided by sessions, cumulative
  • ARPU — revenue divided by players, cumulative
  • ARPPU — revenue divided by paying players, cumulative
  • Plus retention metrics reported with confidence intervals

03Minimum detectable effect

MDE

Check this before you start

The smallest change in your goal metric the experiment can reliably detect, given your sample size.

Calculated from
Goal metric, DAU, rollout percentage, duration, and variant splits
Red flag
An MDE above roughly 100% means significance is effectively unreachable
Hard threshold
Roblox states games under 1,000 DAU might struggle to get useful data
Fix
Larger rollout, longer duration, fewer variants — or accept that this change is not testable yet
If you have fewer than 1,000 DAU

Experiments will mostly not work for you, and pretending otherwise is worse than not testing. Use configs to make changes quickly and reversibly, and evaluate them with before/after cohort comparison instead — ship on a known date, then compare the cohorts who first played in the week before and the week after on the retention dashboard.

It is weaker evidence. It is not no evidence. The discipline that matters is changing one thing at a time and writing down the date.

04Adding experiments to your code

-- Read an experiment value for a specific player.
-- The FIRST GetValue call enrolls that player for that key; every
-- later call returns the same variant for the experiment's duration.

local snapshot = --[[ player-specific ConfigSnapshot ]]
local bossHealth = snapshot:GetValue("bossHealth")

The enrollment rule is worth reading twice: the first GetValue() call on a player-specific snapshot enrolls that player for that key, and only that key. Every subsequent call returns the same value for the duration of the experiment. Only the first call is random.

The practical consequence: where you call GetValue() determines who is in your experiment. If you only read the config inside a shop UI, you have enrolled only players who opened the shop, and your result describes shoppers rather than players. Call it at a point every relevant player reaches.

Targeted experiments

You can restrict an experiment to an audience segment. Two things to know:

  • Narrowing reduces sample size, so MDE rises. Roblox recalculates it using an estimate of the matching audience — watch that number before committing.
  • Enrolment is evaluated per session, but attribution is sticky. A player who matches your criteria mid-session can be enrolled; once enrolled, their data stays attributed to that variant even if they stop matching. D7 retention still counts a player who enrolled on day 0 and returned on day 7, even if they no longer qualify by day 4.

05Reading the results

SignalMeaningAction
Confidence interval excludes 0%Statistically significantThis is a real effect. Consider the trade-offs, then decide
Confidence interval includes 0%Not significantIgnore it. Even a large-looking change
Confidence interval very wideSample too smallThe metric may never reach significance at this scale
One metric up, another downA genuine trade-offDecide whether it is worth it. No tool can answer this for you
Roblox's worked example: D1 retention up 17.4% with bounds of 8.02% and 22.03% — significant, because the interval does not cross zero.

The four discipline rules

  • Let it run the full duration. The novelty effect — temporary interest because something is new, not because it is better — heavily skews early results and can push them in and out of significance. Ending early is how you act on noise.
  • Do not act without significance. Large-looking changes are frequently not significant. If it is not significant, it did not happen.
  • Do not read the results page early except to confirm the experiment is running correctly. Roblox says this explicitly.
  • Balance against qualitative signal. Statistical significance deals in probabilities, not certainties. Weigh results against player feedback and your own vision for the game.

06Making the decision

When an experiment finishes, Make decision starts a guided rollout. Roblox shows any warnings about significance and duration, then asks you to pick a winning variant or keep the control. The chosen value is rolled out to your permanent configs; you can click Change winner if you reconsider.

For an untargeted variant, the value becomes the config's new default and other conditions on that key are removed. For a targeted variant, Roblox integrates the winning rule back into your permanent config — overwriting an identical top condition if one exists, or adding a matching global condition.

07What to test, in order

Given limited traffic and a 14-day minimum, you get a small number of tests per quarter. Spend them on changes where you genuinely cannot predict the answer and where the metric moved is high-tier.

Things not to spend an experiment on

Anything where you already know the answer, anything cosmetic, and anything whose effect is smaller than your MDE. Also: thumbnails. Thumbnail personalisation already runs a continuous multi-armed test for you on the surface that matters — do not duplicate it.
