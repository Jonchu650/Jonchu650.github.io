§ 02  ·  Prioritisation

Every metric, sorted by leverage

Roblox exposes roughly sixty numbers. They are not equally useful, and the dashboard gives you no indication of which are which. Here is the ranking, and the reasoning behind each placement.

The sorting rule: a metric earns a high tier if changing it changes your distribution or your revenue. A metric that only describes a change someone else caused is a diagnostic, however prominently it is displayed.

Tier SMoves distribution

Play through rate · First play bounce rate · Play days per user · Playtime per user · Intentional co-play days · Qualified sessions per user · Spend days per user · Robux spent per user

The eight ranking signals themselves, visible under Acquisition › Home Recommendations. These are the only numbers Roblox has confirmed feed the Recommended for You sort. Everything below exists to explain movement in these.

Note they are measured only on organic Home-recommendation traffic, so they will not match the same-named metrics elsewhere in your dashboard.

Tier AThe two Roblox tells you to fix first

D1 retention · Average session time · Payer conversion rate · ARPPU

These are one step removed from the ranking signals but are the levers Roblox itself names in its official growth sequence. D1 retention and average session time are the pair it says to fix before spending anything on acquisition; payer conversion and ARPPU are the pair it says to fix second.

They are also the metrics with similar-game benchmarks attached, which makes them the only numbers you can meaningfully judge without a baseline of your own.

Tier AStructural health

D7 retention · D30 retention · New user first-session retention · Client crash rate · Out-of-memory exits · ARPDAU

D7 and D30 tell you whether progression and endgame exist. New user first-session retention — the “still playing after X minutes” curve — is the highest-resolution view of your onboarding that Roblox gives you for free, and it maps almost directly onto first play bounce rate.

Crash rate and out-of-memory exits belong in this tier because a crash is indistinguishable from a bounce as far as the algorithm is concerned. A memory regression on low-end Android is a distribution problem wearing an engineering costume.

Tier BDiagnostics — tells you why, not whether

Funnel completion rates · Economy sources & sinks · Average wallet balance · Custom events · Acquisition by source · Play through rate by source · Cohort tables · Demographics · Feedback sentiment · Server FPS · Client FPS · Place-version breakdowns

Enormously valuable, but only once you know which Tier S or A number moved. Opening the funnel dashboard without a question is how afternoons disappear.

Two exceptions worth flagging: the onboarding funnel and the economy sources/sinks balance are diagnostics you should build before you need them, because they are the two that take days of data to become useful.

Tier CContext only — never a target

Total visits · Favourites · Total playtime · Peak CCU · Total revenue · Like ratio · Compute efficiency · MAU

All of these are outputs. None of them is a lever. Optimising a total rather than a per-user average puts you directly at odds with a ranking system that explicitly uses averages so that smaller games are not disadvantaged.

Like ratio is the interesting case: the community widely treats it as a ranking factor, and Roblox has never listed it as one. It genuinely matters — it is right there on your details page, affecting play through rate — but treat it as a conversion input, not an algorithm input. Compute efficiency Roblox states outright is “not indicative of a good or bad player experience.”

01The honest version of the tier list

If you only ever look at four numbers, look at these, in this order:

1First play bounce rate, under-60s bucket
2D1 retention vs. your similar-game benchmark
3Average session time
4Payer conversion rate

The reasoning: bounce rate is the fastest-moving and most fixable of the top-tier signals; D1 and session time are the two Roblox names first and the two with benchmarks; conversion is the monetization metric that moves both revenue and the spend-days signal, because a player who buys once buys again.

One number is not a strategy

These four interact. Cutting your tutorial raises session time and lowers bounce rate but can wreck D7 if players never learn the progression system. Adding a paywall raises conversion and destroys retention. Always check the other three when you move one — Roblox's Experiments tool reports all of them together for exactly this reason.

03Metrics people over-weight

Peak CCU

Tracked obsessively because it is public and comparable. It is a scale readout, not a health readout. Two games at 3,000 CCU with different session lengths have completely different DAU, different ranking signals and different revenue. If you want a scale number, derive DAU from average CCU and daily playtime per user and use that instead.

Total visits

Cumulative and therefore monotonically increasing, which makes it an excellent vanity metric and a useless management one. A game in terminal decline still adds visits every day.

Revenue, in the absence of DAU

Revenue is DAU × conversion × ARPPU. Watching only the product tells you nothing about which term moved. Roblox's own AI weekly reports lead with this decomposition for games over 1,000 DAU; below that threshold you have to do it yourself, and it takes about two minutes.

The similar-games benchmark, as a goal

Benchmarks update daily and your benchmark set can change under you — from all-experiences, to genre, to games-with-similar-players — which will move your apparent percentile without anything about your game changing. The dashboard annotates these transitions on the charts. Read the annotation before you conclude you improved.

The benchmark is a percentile band, not a score

When the dashboard shows a D1 retention benchmark of 12.11%–18.73%, that is the 50th–90th percentile of similar games: half of them are at or below 12.11%, and the top 10% are at or above 18.73%. Landing at 15% means you are comfortably above median, not that you are failing to reach 18.73%.
