§ 03  ·  Metrics

Engagement

How much of a player's attention you actually hold. Three of the eight ranking signals live here, and it is the half of the retention/engagement pair that you can move in a single update.

02What good looks like

The 10-minute line

Creator Rewards pays 5 Robux per day for each Active Spender who plays your game for 10+ minutes, provided yours is one of the first three experiences they launch that day. Roblox's own documentation tells you to check whether your average playtime is near or below that threshold, because if it is, most of your qualifying players never qualify.

That makes 10 minutes a hard, non-arbitrary target for median session length — not average, median. It is one of the few places on Roblox where a specific number has money attached to it.

03Moving average session time

Roblox's guidance splits cleanly into two halves: get them into the fun immediately, and give them a reason to come back. The first half is what moves session time and bounce rate.

Get into the fun immediately

The friction of joining and leaving a Roblox game is close to zero, which makes first impressions disproportionately valuable. Roblox's three rules:

  • Fun within five minutes. Whatever makes your game distinctive should be communicated inside the first five minutes — not described, experienced.
  • No long tutorials. Make learning contextual and action-based: short unobtrusive prompts when a player first meets a feature, tooltips in the shop. Give starter items and currency so the core loop is reachable immediately.
  • Remove barriers to social interaction. Group play and social contact are core to how successful Roblox games work; design for them rather than around them.

Give a reason to come back

  • Clear goals at three time horizons. Short-term for instant gratification, mid-term for direction, long-term for aspiration — completing a collection, hitting the top rank, landing a rare item.
  • A progression system that makes existing content last longer, buying you time to build more.
  • Progression that does not gate the fun. Enough content must be available upfront that a brand-new player still has a good first session.
The single highest-leverage session-time change

Find the distribution, not the average. Export your session data, or use a custom event logging session length, and look at the bottom quartile. Almost every game has a cluster of sessions under two minutes; almost every such cluster has one cause, and it is usually load time, a confusing first screen, or a device-specific failure. Fixing it moves the average, the bounce rate and the qualified-session signal simultaneously.

04Moving play days per user

Play days is a separate problem from session time, and the solutions barely overlap. Session time is about the current visit. Play days is about the gap between visits.

05Performance is an engagement metric

Roblox lists performance under engagement guidance, and the ranking mechanics explain why: a crash and a bounce are the same event from the algorithm's perspective. Test across devices and platforms, limit excessive textures and meshes, and run a community that will report bugs and crashes you cannot reproduce.

See Performance for the specific thresholds — client CPU under 16 ms for 60 FPS, server memory under 3 GB, and the memory-group breakdown that finds the devices failing in your game.

06Diagnosing a session-time drop

  1. Check the date against your releases. Use the Performance dashboard's place-version breakdown to compare the new build against previous ones directly.
  2. Check the error and performance reports. A regression in crash rate or out-of-memory exits will present as a session-time drop.
  3. Break down by platform and OS. Mobile-only regressions are common and invisible in the aggregate. Roblox also offers a memory-group breakdown (<2 GB, 2–4 GB, 4–8 GB, >8 GB) for exactly this.
  4. Break down by When user first played. If only the 0–30 day cohort dropped, you changed onboarding. If only older cohorts dropped, you changed something veterans relied on.
  5. Check the new user first session retention curve against the previous period. It will tell you which minute you started losing people.
  6. Only then open the onboarding funnel to find the specific step.
